## 0. How to Use This Manual

This is how we build and how we act on site. It's written for a new carpenter or cabinetmaker on day one, and it's the standard for everyone else too.

The employee handbook covers pay, leave, safety rules and the rest of the HR side. This manual covers the work. If the two ever disagree, the handbook wins. Tell Katrina so we can fix it.

A few things to know:

- These are standards, not suggestions. Not following them may lead to discipline.
- No manual covers everything. When it doesn't say, use judgment and ask.
- This manual isn't a contract. Sparrow can change it at any time.
- Got a better way to do something? Tell Derek or your super. That's how this manual gets better.

## 1. The Sparrow Standard

Our work is defect-free and built to last. That's the whole goal. Everything else in this manual supports it.

- **Build it right.** If you're not sure how to build something so it's defect-free and long-lasting, ask before you build it.
- **The safest way is the right way.** Never run a machine or make a cut you're not comfortable with.
- **Own your work.** Spotting defects and fixing them is part of your job. If something could hurt the finished product, find it and fix it, or flag it.
- **Protect the home.** We work in people's houses. Treat every one like it's finished, even when it isn't.
- **Leave it better.** Clean up every day. Leave the site and the shop the way you'd want to find them.

## 2. Safety in the Shop and Field

The full safety rules are in handbook Section 16 and Sparrow's Accident Prevention Program. Know them. The short version:

- Safety comes before schedule, every time.
- Anyone can stop work if something is unsafe. No one gets in trouble for it.
- Safety glasses in all work areas. Hearing protection with any machine.
- Don't run a shop machine until you've been trained and signed off on it.
- If a machine isn't safe, tag it out and tell Derek or your super.
- Report every injury and near-miss, no matter how small.

## 3. Shop Standards

### 3.1 Training and sign-off

Every new shop or field employee gets trained on our shop machines and on this manual at hire. You'll be signed off one machine at a time. Until you're signed off on a machine, don't run it alone.

**[CONFIRM]** List of machines that need sign-off, and who signs off (Derek only, or Derek and a lead).

### 3.2 Read the plans. Check the plans.

Plans have mistakes. Catching them is part of the job.

- Verify dimensions before you cut. Check field measurements against the drawings.
- Check the math. Add up the run. Make sure the parts actually fit the space.
- Make sure it can be installed. Will it fit through the door, up the stairs, around the corner? Can it be scribed and fastened?
- Found a problem? Stop and tell Derek or the PM before you build. Don't guess.

### 3.3 Material selection

- Look at every board before it goes in a part.
- Discard and replace any warped, twisted or defective wood. Don't try to make it work.
- Pick grain and color for the location. Best faces go where people see them.

**[CONFIRM]** Do we check moisture content on incoming lumber? Target range?

### 3.4 Millwork assembly

- Floating tenons are required in all pre-finished joints.
- Dominos are required at all face frame, casing, baseboard and moulding joints.
- If a domino isn't possible, use a stronger connection than an end-grain glue joint. Ask if you're not sure what that is.
- Glue-ups clean and flush. Remove squeeze-out before it skins over.

**[CONFIRM]** Are "floating tenons" and "dominos" the same thing in our shop? Standard domino sizes by stock thickness?

## 5. Packing, Delivery and Receiving

### 5.1 Packing and loading

- Wrap and protect all finished work before it leaves the shop.
- Label every piece (see 3.6).
- Load so nothing shifts. Strap it. Pad it.
- Bring the hardware, fasteners, fillers and scribes with the job. Check them off before you leave.

### 5.2 Delivering to a client home

- Protect the home during every delivery. Floors, door jambs, walls, stair treads.
- Walk the path before you carry anything. Move nothing of the client's. If something's in the way, ask the client to move it.
- If something seems questionable, stop and find a better way. Get another person. Take it apart. Don't force it.
- Offer to spot drivers in and out of driveways.

### 5.3 Receiving goods

When a delivery shows up, at the shop or on site:

- Check it against the packing list before the driver leaves.
- If anything is damaged or unusable, photograph everything. Make the driver aware and note it on the delivery slip.
- Tell management right away. For the shop that's Derek. On site it's your super or PM.
- Don't install damaged material.

## 6. Installation and Onsite Carpentry

### 6.1 Before you start

- Read the drawings and know the scope for the day.
- Set up floor and wall protection first. **[CONFIRM]** Standard protection product and whether booties are required.
- Check the site. Walls, floors and openings against the drawings. Flag anything that doesn't match before you install.
- Set up the laser. Every install references the laser level provided.

### 6.2 Cabinets

- All cabinets plumb and level to the laser provided. No exceptions.
- No tool belts while installing cabinetry. Use a vest or apron. Belts scratch boxes and finished faces.
- Never stand drills on counters or floors. Lay them on their side.
- Never set tools, materials or work on an unprotected client surface. Not even if the homeowner says it's OK.

### 6.3 Scribes and caulk

- Scribes within one veneer thickness at finished surfaces. **[CONFIRM]** the number.
- Scribes within 1/16" if the work gets painted onsite after install.
- White-to-white scribes get caulked before you leave the site for the day. **[CONFIRM]** exactly which joints "white-to-white" covers.

### 6.4 Doors, drawers and hardware

- Adjust doors and drawers so reveals are even and everything opens and closes clean.
- If doors and drawers aren't adjusted yet, don't leave them hung overnight. Take them down and store them protected.

**[CONFIRM]** Target reveal or gap for doors and drawers.

### 6.5 End of every day

- Clean the work area. **[CONFIRM]** broom clean or vacuumed.
- Caulk white-to-white scribes.
- Take down unadjusted doors and drawers.
- Pack out all trash and food waste.
- Leave protection in place and in good shape.
- Lock up the way the client or super asked.

### 6.6 Final day

- Wipe all cabinet interiors, drawers and exteriors with a damp rag.
- Final adjust of every door and drawer.
- Check every scribe, caulk line and fastener hole.
- Remove all protection, tape and labels, unless the super says to leave protection for other trades.
- Walk it with your super. Defect-free is the goal.

## 7. Working in Client Homes

Clients let us into their homes. That's trust, and we keep it.

### 7.1 Respect the home

- Never touch or move client property. Ask the client to move it.
- Never set anything on an unprotected client surface. Not even if they say it's fine.
- Bring all your own tools and equipment. Never ask to borrow anything, not even a broom or an extension cord.

**[CONFIRM]** Bathroom use. Can crews use the client's bathroom, or is there always another plan?

### 7.2 Food and drink

- No open beverages in any home. Capped water bottles are OK. Cans and coffee cups aren't.
- Eat outside or in the area your super sets up.
- Pack out all food waste. Don't use the client's trash.

### 7.3 Noise and music

- Music at low volume.
- Family-friendly at all times.
- Keep language clean. Clients, kids and neighbors can hear you.

### 7.4 Talking with clients

- Be friendly and professional.
- Questions about scope, schedule, cost or changes go to your super or PM. Don't agree to changes yourself, even small ones. **[CONFIRM]**
- If a client asks you to do something outside the scope, say "Let me check with my super" and then check.

## 8. Tools and Equipment

### 8.1 Your tools

Carpenters and cabinetmakers bring their own hand tools, a tool belt or apron, and a basic cordless kit. The tool allowance is in handbook Section 19.

- Mark every personal tool.
- Keep them clean, sharp and working.
- If a job needs a tool you don't have, tell your super ahead of time.

### 8.2 Sparrow tools and equipment

- Treat them like your own, or better.
- Return them clean, charged and complete.
- Report damage or problems right away. Don't put a broken tool back on the shelf.

### 8.3 Care and sharpening

- Replace dull carbide right away.
- Keep chisels and plane irons sharp.
- Clean pitch off blades and bits.
- Check cords and guards before each use.

## 10. When Something Goes Wrong

Mistakes happen. Hiding them is the problem. Speak up early and we'll fix it together.

- **You find a defect in your work.** Fix it. If you can't, flag it to Derek or your super.
- **You find a mistake on the plans.** Stop and tell the PM before you build or install.
- **You damage something in a client home.** Stop. Tell your super right away. Don't try to hide it or fix it on your own. Photograph it.
- **A delivery arrives damaged.** Follow 5.3.
- **Someone gets hurt or almost gets hurt.** Follow handbook Section 16.

## 12. Sparrow Details

**NOTE:** Long-term project. This section is a placeholder. Details get added over time, about one a month.

Sections 1 through 11 cover how we work. Sparrow Details covers how we build specific things. One page per detail, with photos from our own jobs.

### Ground rules

- Manufacturer installation instructions and the architect's drawings and specs always come first.
- If a detail here doesn't match the drawings, talk to your PM. Any change to the architect's design goes through an RFI or other written approval.
- Field conditions won't always match the photos. If you have to adapt a detail, ask your super or Derek first.
- These are our details, built from our own work. Don't copy another company's drawings or text into this section.

### How a detail gets made

1. Pick the detail. Start with what shows up in callbacks and warranty work.
2. The next time a crew builds it on a job, the super takes 8 to 10 photos, one per step.
3. Derek or the super writes the captions. Katrina formats the page.
4. The owner reviews it with the crew that built it, then it goes in the manual.
5. Every detail gets reviewed once a year, or sooner if something fails.
