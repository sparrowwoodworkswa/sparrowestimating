# Employee Handbook: Washington Compliance Review

**Prepared for:** Mike Stuntz
**Date:** November 22, 2026
**What I reviewed:**
1. Original draft, "SW_Employee Handbook - DRAFT" (the Boston-based copy), plus all 55 comment threads on it.
2. Katrina's rewrite, "Employee Handbook Sparrow Woodworks 2026-v2" (Version 2, 7/2026).
3. Katrina's list of red-flagged changes and new sections.

**Note:** This is a compliance read, not legal advice. Washington has some specific traps. Before you roll this out, have a Washington employment attorney sign off on the final version. Expect about an hour of their time, since most of the work is done.

---

## Bottom line

Katrina's v2 is a big improvement. It's shorter, cleaner, and she caught most of the Massachusetts carryover. But some Massachusetts rules are still in v2, and a few of them break Washington law. Fix these before anyone signs it:

1. **Meal breaks are wrong, and rest breaks are missing.** "Six hours" is the Massachusetts rule. In Washington, the meal break kicks in after **5 hours**. Employees also get a **paid 10-minute rest break for every 4 hours worked**. This is the biggest exposure, because it hits every field employee every day.
2. **Sick leave carryover "one year only, use it or lose it" isn't allowed.** Every year, up to 40 unused sick hours must roll over. You can't expire them after year two.
3. **PFML says "26 weeks."** That's Massachusetts. Washington allows 12 weeks family or medical, 16 combined, and 18 with a pregnancy complication.
4. **Job protection starts January 1, 2027.** Starting then, Washington PFML job protection covers employers with **15 or more employees**. At about 22 people, Sparrow is covered. Anyone with 180 days on the job gets their job back after PFML, and you have to keep their health insurance going while they're out. The 8-week "Parental Leave" section is the Massachusetts Parental Leave Act. Replace it.
5. **The harassment complaint deadline is wrong.** "300 days" is the federal EEOC deadline, not the state Human Rights Commission's. List both agencies.
6. **"Federal and state taxes"** should go. Washington has no state income tax. The deductions list is also missing PFML premiums, WA Cares, and the employee share of L&I.
7. **The confidentiality section says "no exceptions."** You need a carve-out. Washington's Silenced No More Act and federal labor law protect employees who talk about wages, harassment, discrimination, or safety.
8. **Drop the new post-employment client restriction.** It's a non-compete. Under current law it's unenforceable for almost anyone at Sparrow. Washington bans nearly all non-competes as of June 30, 2027, and trying to enforce one costs you a $5,000 penalty plus attorney fees.

The rest is cleanup, missing sections, and decisions only you can make. Details are below.

## 2. Should fix (risk, not clear violations)

| Section | Issue | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Tool allowance and equipment damage | "Tools over $500 will be deducted from your paycheck" and "required reimbursement" for damage. Washington allows deductions only with the employee's specific advance agreement (RCW 49.48.010, WAC 296-126-028). They can't take a non-exempt employee below minimum wage, and they can break salary basis for exempt staff. | Have each employee sign a short **deduction authorization** at hire covering tool overages and unreturned property. Don't deduct for accidental damage. Reserve that for willful or grossly negligent damage, and even then check first. |
| Direct deposit | Original v2 edits made it "required." | I couldn't confirm a clear Washington rule either way. The safe move is "Direct deposit is our standard. Fill out the authorization form with Katrina." Keep a paper check available on request. |
| "Leads must submit time by Monday or risk missing the pay cycle" | Washington requires paying all wages earned on the regular payday. A late timesheet doesn't let you hold pay. | Reword: "Submit by end of day Monday so payroll is accurate." Pay late entries on the regular payday anyway, using the best info you have. |
| Cell phone allowance: "must answer calls after hours" | For **non-exempt** staff, after-hours calls and texts are paid work time and count toward overtime. | Add: "Non-exempt employees must record after-hours work time, including calls and texts." |
| Company vehicles taken home | In *Stevens v. Brink's* (Wash. 2007), the court held that drive time in a company vehicle from home to the first job was paid time when the employee was under company rules and hauling company tools. Washington has no federal-style Portal-to-Portal exception. | Talk to your attorney about how trucks are assigned. At minimum, know this exposure exists for anyone who takes a truck home with tools. |
| Vehicle cameras | "Front-facing cameras." Washington is an **all-party consent** state for recording conversations (RCW 9.73.030). | If the cameras record cab audio, say so in the handbook and get a signed acknowledgment. Video only is lower risk. Disclose it anyway. |
| "All employees must have a valid driver's license" | That's overbroad for jobs that don't need driving. It can screen out people with disabilities (ADA/WLAD). | "Employees in positions that require driving must..." |
| Texting while driving | Only covers company vehicles. | Washington's E-DUI law covers any vehicle. Apply the rule to personal vehicles on company business too. |
| Outside Employment Policy | RCW 49.62.070: you can't stop employees earning under 2x minimum wage (about $34/hr in 2026, $35.46/hr in 2027) from holding a second job or being self-employed. The exceptions are safety, schedule interference, and the duty of loyalty and conflicts of interest. Banning side work on vacation days, for example, goes too far. | Keep: disclosure, no company tools/time/info, no competing for Sparrow's clients or bids, and no side work while on sick leave (that's misuse). Drop the blanket ban on side work during vacation. Keep fitness-for-duty only when based on objective evidence. |
| Workers' comp | "The Company carries workers' comp insurance." Washington is a state-fund state (L&I). "Failure to report may affect eligibility" can read like discouraging claims (RCW 51.48.025). | "Sparrow is covered by the Washington L&I state fund. Report injuries right away so we can help with your claim. Sparrow will never retaliate for filing a claim." Claims must be filed within 1 year (2 years for occupational disease). |
| Safety: "OSHA" throughout | Washington runs its own program: WISHA, enforced by L&I DOSH. | Replace "OSHA" with "L&I / DOSH." See Section 3 for additions. |
| Background checks | Missing the Fair Chance Act (RCW 49.94: no criminal history questions until the applicant is otherwise qualified) and credit check limits (RCW 19.182.020: only if job-related, with the reason given in writing). Federal FCRA disclosure applies if you use a screening company. | Add one sentence: "Background checks follow federal and Washington law, including the Washington Fair Chance Act." |
| Non-solicitation ("unless approved by Michael Stuntz") | Requiring approval invites claims of selective enforcement if it's ever used against union talk. | Either drop "unless approved" or apply it the same to every cause, including charity drives. |
| Shop use after hours | v2 says the shop is "available to all employees" and also that it rents for "$15/hr with permission." Those conflict. Off-duty personal projects aren't covered by L&I, so an injury there is a liability problem. | Pick one rule. Require a signed waiver and a training sign-off, and keep anyone from running high-risk machines alone. Call your insurance agent before you allow it. |
| Mileage vs. vehicle allowance | v2 has IRS-rate mileage with logs, a "monthly allowance," *and* "add it to your timesheet for reimbursement." That's three systems. | Pick one and delete the other two. |
| Shop and install standards | They carry discipline promises ("grounds for a formal written warning"). In *Thompson v. St. Regis* (Wash. 1984), the court held that specific handbook promises can be binding. | Agree with Katrina: move them to a Shop and Field Standards manual. Keep "may result in discipline" in the handbook. |

### Suggested rewrite: Client Relationships

> **Client Relationships**
> While you work at Sparrow, you may not do construction, remodel, or trade work for any client with an active Sparrow project or open Sparrow bid, unless it's part of your Sparrow job. That includes side jobs, cash jobs, and "favors."
>
> If a current or past Sparrow client asks you about trade work, tell Mike before you answer. We want the chance to take the job. If we pass, we'll tell you in writing whether you're free to take it.
>
> Non-trade favors like pet sitting or house sitting are fine.
>
> Breaking this policy may lead to discipline, up to termination.

Why: an employee taking work away from an active Sparrow client is a duty-of-loyalty problem. Washington's moonlighting law leaves that alone. A 12-month look-back and any after-you-leave restriction go further than Washington will enforce. If you want real post-employment protection, ask your attorney about a narrow **customer non-solicitation agreement**. Washington still allows those in limited form after 2027. It belongs in a separate signed agreement, not the handbook.

## 6. Sample language for the big fixes

**Meal and Rest Breaks**
> Non-exempt employees get a paid 10-minute rest break for every 4 hours worked, as close to the middle of each 4 hours as practical. No one should work more than 3 hours without a rest break.
>
> If you work more than 5 hours, you get an unpaid 30-minute meal break, starting no earlier than your 2nd hour and no later than your 5th. If you work 3 or more hours past your normal shift, you get a second 30-minute meal break. You must be fully relieved of duty during a meal break. If you're asked to stay on duty, the break is paid. Record your meal break on your timesheet.

**Washington Paid Sick Leave**
> All non-exempt employees, including part-time and temporary staff, earn 1 hour of paid sick leave for every 40 hours worked, starting on day one. You can use it starting on your 90th day. Up to 40 unused hours carry over each year. Unused sick leave is not paid out when you leave. If you're rehired within 12 months, your balance comes back.
>
> You can use sick leave for your own or a family member's illness, injury, or medical care, including preventive care. Family includes child, parent, spouse or domestic partner, grandparent, grandchild, sibling, and anyone who lives with you and depends on your care. You can also use it for domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking; when your workplace or your child's school or care is closed by a public health or emergency order; for immigration proceedings for you or a family member; and for needs related to being a hate crime victim.
>
> Give 10 days' notice when you can. Otherwise, let your supervisor know as soon as possible. We may ask for verification only if you're out more than 3 workdays in a row. Using sick leave as allowed will never count against you.

**Paid Family and Medical Leave**
> Washington PFML is a state insurance program. You and Sparrow both pay premiums through payroll. Eligible employees can get partial wage replacement from the state for up to 12 weeks of family leave or 12 weeks of medical leave, 16 weeks combined, and 18 if there's a pregnancy complication. Family leave covers bonding with a new child, caring for a seriously ill family member, and certain military family needs.
>
> Starting January 1, 2027, if you've worked here at least 180 days, your job is protected while you're on PFML, and Sparrow keeps your health insurance going on the same terms. Apply at paidleave.wa.gov. Tell Katrina as early as you can, ideally 30 days ahead for planned leave.

**Your Rights (add to Confidentiality and Social Media)**
> Nothing in this handbook stops you from talking about your wages, hours, or working conditions with coworkers or anyone else. Nothing stops you from reporting or discussing harassment, discrimination, retaliation, wage-and-hour violations, sexual assault, or safety concerns, or from filing a complaint with a government agency. Sparrow will never ask for your personal social media passwords or ask you to add a manager to your accounts.

## 7. Decisions only you can make

1. **Work hours:** 7-5 or 8-5?
2. **PTO tiers:** keep 40/80, or add a 5-year (or 8- or 10-year) tier?
3. **PTO payout when someone leaves:** v2 says yes. Kari's older notes say use-it-or-lose-it with no carryover. Both are legal. The handbook just has to say one thing, because a written payout promise is enforceable.
4. **School-function leave (24 hrs):** keep it as a perk or cut it?
5. **Retirement plan:** 401(k) or SIMPLE IRA? Is dental (Delta Dental) still offered? v2 dropped it.
6. **Mileage:** IRS rate with logs, or a monthly allowance?
7. **After-hours shop use:** allow it, and is it free or $15/hr?
8. **Truck take-home policy:** worth a call with your attorney given the drive-time case.
