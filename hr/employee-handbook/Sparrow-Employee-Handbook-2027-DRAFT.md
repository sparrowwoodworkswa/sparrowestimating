## 1. About Sparrow

Welcome to Sparrow Woodworks. We're a general contractor and custom millwork shop in Indianola, building since 2011. Our mission is to build better homes, relationships and futures.

**Build Better Homes.** Quality work, sustainable practices and craftsmanship that lasts.

**Build Better Relationships.** Transparency, accountability and clear communication with clients, coworkers, vendors and subs.

**Build Better Futures.** Growth for our people, better lives for our clients, and long-term partnerships.

## 2. About This Handbook

This handbook explains how we work and what you can expect from Sparrow. Read it and keep it handy. It replaces all earlier handbooks and policies.

We can't cover every situation. Sparrow may change these policies at any time and will let you know when we do. Benefit plan documents control over anything in this handbook.

**This handbook is not a contract.** Employment at Sparrow is at-will. You or Sparrow can end it at any time, for any reason, with or without notice. Only Michael Stuntz can change that, in a written agreement he signs.

**Open door.** If you have a question or a problem, talk to your supervisor or Mike. If the problem involves Mike, talk to any project manager or Katrina Diller. You will never be punished for raising a concern in good faith.

## 3. Equal Opportunity and Respectful Workplace

### Equal opportunity

Sparrow does not discriminate based on race (including hair texture and protective hairstyles), color, creed, religion, sex, pregnancy, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, marital status, national origin, ancestry, citizenship or immigration status, age, disability, use of a service animal, genetic information, military or veteran status, or any other status protected by law. This applies to hiring, pay, promotion, discipline, firing and every other part of the job.

### Harassment and bullying

We don't tolerate harassment or bullying from anyone, including coworkers, supervisors, clients, vendors or subs.

Harassment is unwelcome conduct tied to any protected status above. **Sexual harassment** includes unwelcome advances, requests for sexual favors, and sexual jokes, comments, images or touching, when it's tied to a job decision or makes the workplace hostile. **Bullying** is repeated abuse such as threats, insults, ridicule or humiliation.

### How to report

Report it, verbally or in writing, to your supervisor, Mike or Katrina. You can also skip the chain and go straight to Mike. We'll look into every report promptly and keep it as confidential as we can. If we find a problem, we'll fix it and take action, up to termination.

**No retaliation.** No one will be punished for reporting in good faith or helping with an investigation. Retaliation is itself a violation of this policy.

You can also contact a government agency at any time, whether or not you report to us first. Filing deadlines apply, so act promptly.
- **Washington State Human Rights Commission:** 800-233-3247, hum.wa.gov
- **U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission:** 800-669-4000, eeoc.gov

## 4. Accommodations

Sparrow follows the ADA, the Washington Law Against Discrimination and the Healthy Starts Act. We provide reasonable accommodations for disability, pregnancy, lactation and religious practices unless it would cause undue hardship.

To ask, talk to Mike. We'll meet with you, talk through options, and get back to you quickly.

**Pregnancy.** We won't ask for a doctor's note for more frequent restroom, food or water breaks; seating; limits on lifting 17 pounds or more; or private space to express milk. You won't be forced to take leave if another accommodation works.

**Lactation.** For up to two years after a child's birth, you get reasonable break time to express milk, in a private space that isn't a bathroom.

## 6. Hours, Breaks and Timekeeping

### Hours and attendance
Work hours depend on the job site. They usually fall between 7:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m., and a full workday is 8 hours. Your supervisor will set your schedule for each site.

- If you'll be late or out, tell your supervisor at least 60 minutes before your start time, or as soon as you can.
- Schedule appointments outside work hours when you can.
- Repeated lateness or unexcused absences may lead to discipline.
- Three workdays in a row with no contact is treated as quitting.

None of this applies to time covered by sick leave or other protected leave.

### Breaks (non-exempt employees)

**Rest breaks are paid.** You get a paid 10-minute rest break for every 4 hours worked, as close to the middle of that 4 hours as practical. Don't go more than 3 hours without one.

**Lunch is unpaid.** If you work more than 5 hours, you get a 30-minute meal break. Lunch is unpaid break time. It's not counted as hours worked, and it's in addition to your 8 hours. Take it no earlier than your 2nd hour and no later than your 5th. If you work 3 or more hours past your normal shift, you get a second 30-minute unpaid meal break.

During lunch you're fully off duty. No work, calls or texts. If a supervisor asks you to work through lunch or stay on duty, that time is paid and you should record it as hours worked.

### Timekeeping
- Non-exempt employees record all time worked in the Company's timekeeping system. On field crews, the site superintendent may enter time. Check that it's right.
- **Never work off the clock.** If anyone asks you to, tell Mike.
- Exempt employees record billable hours and track PTO and sick time.
- Leads submit crew time by end of day Monday so payroll is accurate.
- Falsifying time, or entering time for someone else, is grounds for termination.
- If your time or pay looks wrong, tell Katrina. We'll fix it, and you won't be punished for asking.

## 7. Pay

### Payday
The workweek runs Sunday 12:00 a.m. through Saturday 11:59 p.m. You're paid every Friday. If Friday is a holiday, you're paid the business day before. Direct deposit is our standard. Set it up with Katrina.

### Overtime
Non-exempt employees are paid 1.5 times their regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek. Paid leave and holidays don't count toward the 40. Overtime must be approved by your supervisor in advance, but all hours worked will be paid.

### Deductions
Required deductions are federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare, Washington Paid Family and Medical Leave premiums, WA Cares (long-term care) premiums, and the employee share of L&I workers' comp. Other deductions, like benefit premiums or tool overages, are only taken with your written authorization or a court order. Every deduction shows on your pay stub. Questions about a deduction go to Jarad Clark at [email]. If a deduction was wrong, we'll pay it back.

## 8. Paid Time Off and Holidays

### PTO (vacation and personal time)

Full-time employees earn PTO on every hour worked:

| Years of service | Accrual | About | Max balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 2 years | 0.04 hrs per hour worked | 40 hrs/year | 40 hrs |
| 2 years and up | 0.08 hrs per hour worked | 80 hrs/year | 80 hrs |

- Once you hit the max balance, you stop earning PTO until you use some.
- PTO does not carry over. Use it by December 31.
- Request PTO from your supervisor at least two weeks ahead. Approval depends on workload. Senior staff should line up coverage when they ask.
- Minimum use is 1 hour.
- PTO is paid at your base rate for the hours you would have worked.
- PTO can't take you over 40 paid hours in a week. Unused hours go back in your bank.
- Unused PTO is paid out when you leave Sparrow.
- Sick leave is a separate bank. See Section 9.

### Paid holidays

New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve and Christmas Day.

- Full-time: 8 hours of holiday pay. Part-time: 4 hours.
- A holiday on a weekend is observed on the nearest weekday.
- Non-exempt employees must work their scheduled day before and after the holiday to get holiday pay. Approved PTO, sick leave and other protected leave count as working. This rule doesn't apply to exempt employees.
- A holiday during your vacation is paid as a holiday, not PTO.
- We'll work with you on time off for other religious holidays, using PTO or unpaid time.

## 9. Washington Paid Sick Leave

**Who:** All employees, including part-time and temporary.

**How much:** 1 hour of sick leave for every 40 hours worked, starting day one (about 52 hours a year full-time).

**When you can use it:** Starting your 90th day.

**What it's for:**
- Your own or a family member's illness, injury, medical care or preventive care. Family means child, parent, spouse or domestic partner, grandparent, grandchild, sibling, and anyone who lives with you and depends on you for care.
- Domestic violence, sexual assault or stalking.
- When your workplace, or your child's school or care, is closed by a public health or emergency order.
- Immigration proceedings for you or a family member.
- Needs related to being the victim of a hate crime.

**Carryover:** Up to 40 unused hours carry over to the next year, every year.

**Notice:** For planned leave, give 10 days' notice when you can. Otherwise, tell your supervisor as soon as possible, before your shift if you can.

**Doctor's notes:** Only required if you're out more than 3 workdays in a row.

**Pay:** Paid at your normal rate for the hours you miss. Unused sick leave isn't paid out when you leave. If you're rehired within 12 months, your balance comes back.

**No retaliation:** Using sick leave the way the law allows will never count against you. Your balance is shown on your pay stub.

## 10. Other Leave

### Paid Family and Medical Leave (PFML)
Washington PFML is a state insurance program. You and Sparrow both pay into it. Eligible employees get partial pay from the state for up to:
- 12 weeks for your own serious health condition,
- 12 weeks to bond with a new child, care for a seriously ill family member, or handle certain military family needs,
- 16 weeks combined, or 18 if there's a pregnancy complication.

If you've worked at Sparrow at least 180 days, your job is protected. When you come back, you return to your job or an equivalent one, and Sparrow keeps your health insurance going on the same terms while you're out. Apply at paidleave.wa.gov. Tell Katrina as early as you can, ideally 30 days ahead for planned leave.

### Pregnancy disability leave
Separate from PFML, you can take leave for the time your doctor says you're disabled by pregnancy or childbirth. Your job is protected when you return.

### Domestic violence leave
You can take reasonable time off, paid or unpaid, to deal with domestic violence, sexual assault or stalking affecting you or a family member. You can use sick leave or PTO.

### Bereavement
After 90 days, full-time employees get paid time off for a death in the family:
- Up to 3 days: spouse or domestic partner, child, parent, sibling, grandparent or grandchild.
- 1 day: aunt, uncle, niece, nephew or in-law.

You can add PTO if you need more time.

### Jury duty
Full-time and part-time employees get their regular base pay for the first 3 days of jury service. Tell your supervisor within 48 hours of getting a summons. Come back to work if you're excused during the workday. We may ask for proof of service. Sparrow will never penalize you for serving.

### Military leave
Sparrow grants leave for service in the U.S. Armed Forces, Reserves or National Guard, as required by USERRA and Washington law. If your spouse is deployed or home on leave from deployment, you can take up to 15 days of unpaid leave. Tell Mike as early as you can.

## 13. Conflicts of Interest

Put Sparrow's interests first on Sparrow's time. Tell Mike about any actual or possible conflict, such as a financial interest in a client, vendor or competitor, or supervising a family member.

### Client relationships
While you work at Sparrow, don't do construction, remodel or trade work for any client with an active Sparrow project or open bid, except as part of your Sparrow job. That includes side jobs, cash jobs and favors.

If a current or past Sparrow client asks you about trade work, tell Mike before you answer. We want the chance to take the job. If we pass, we'll tell you in writing whether you're free to take it. Non-trade favors like pet sitting or house sitting are fine.

### Outside work
You're free to work outside Sparrow on your own time. Tell your supervisor in writing if it's construction or trade work. Outside work can't:
- interfere with your Sparrow schedule or job performance,
- create a safety risk, including showing up too worn out to work safely,
- use Sparrow tools, vehicles, materials, time or confidential information,
- compete for Sparrow's clients or bids.

Don't do outside work while on sick leave. Injuries on an outside job aren't covered by Sparrow's workers' comp.

### Gifts
Don't give or accept gifts, favors or entertainment from clients, vendors, subs or competitors in connection with your work, beyond small things like a business meal. Report any gift worth more than $75 to Mike.

## 14. Confidentiality, Social Media and Your Rights

### Confidentiality
Keep client information private. That includes names, addresses, plans, photos, finances and anything about their homes and families. Don't share Sparrow's financial or proprietary business information outside the Company without permission.

### Social media
- Don't post client information, or photos of client homes, without Sparrow's approval.
- Don't speak for Sparrow unless Mike says you can. If you post about work, make it clear the views are your own.
- Don't post content that's threatening, harassing or discriminatory, or that you know is false.
- Don't use your Sparrow email for personal accounts.

### Your rights
Nothing in this handbook stops you from:
- talking about your pay, hours or working conditions with coworkers or anyone else,
- reporting or discussing harassment, discrimination, retaliation, wage problems, sexual assault or safety concerns,
- filing a complaint with, or talking to, a government agency.

Sparrow will never ask for your personal social media passwords or ask you to add a manager to your accounts.

## 15. Phones, Equipment and Monitoring

### Personal cell phones
Most of us use our phones for work, and that's fine. But personal use adds up fast, and on a jobsite it can get someone hurt.

- **Personal use:** Keep personal calls, texts, social media and browsing to breaks and lunch. Short calls for something urgent, like family, a kid's school or a doctor, are OK. Step away and keep it brief. Repeated personal use on the clock may lead to discipline.
- **Safety:** No phone use while running a machine or power tool, on a ladder or scaffold, or while being spotted. No earbuds or headphones on a jobsite or in the shop while machines are running, except approved hearing protection.
- **Driving:** No handheld phone use while driving a company vehicle, or your own vehicle on company business. It's also Washington law. Hands-free only when safe.
- **Client homes:** Keep calls quiet and short inside a client's home. Take personal calls outside. Work photos for documentation are encouraged. No personal photos of a client's home, family or belongings.
- **Phone allowance:** Keep your phone charged and answer work calls during work hours. Non-exempt employees: after-hours work calls, texts and email are paid time. Record them.

### Company equipment
Phones, laptops, vehicles, tools and other equipment Sparrow issues stay Sparrow's property. Take care of them, report damage or problems right away, and return everything when you leave. Careless or improper use may lead to discipline. Deductions for unreturned or willfully damaged property are only made with your signed authorization.

### Monitoring
Company vehicles have GPS mileage tracking and cameras. Company email, laptops, phones and accounts belong to Sparrow and may be monitored. Don't expect privacy when using them. You'll sign an acknowledgment of this.

## 16. Safety and Injuries

Safety comes before schedule, every time.

**Stop-work authority.** Anyone can stop work if something is unsafe. No one gets in trouble for it.

### Rules
- Safety glasses at all times in all work areas. Hearing protection with any stationary or handheld machine.
- Hard hats, face protection and other PPE as required by L&I. Sparrow provides it.
- Work boots on jobsites. Sneakers are OK in finished homes.
- Never move material or tools in a way that puts someone at risk.
- Never leave a machine in an unsafe state. If it's not safe to use, tag it out and tell your supervisor.
- Use extreme care on ladders and around anyone on a ladder.
- Never make a cut or run a machine you're not comfortable with. Ask.
- Keep fire exits, extinguishers and walkways clear. Store flammables in closed metal containers.
- Follow Sparrow's Accident Prevention Program, attend crew safety meetings, and follow L&I rules for outdoor heat and wildfire smoke.

### Injuries
1. Get first aid or call 911 if needed.
2. Tell your supervisor and Katrina right away, no matter how minor. Fill out an incident report.
3. Report near-misses and hazards too.

Sparrow is covered by the Washington L&I state fund. You have the right to file a claim, and Sparrow will never retaliate against you for it. Claims must be filed within 1 year of the injury. When possible, we'll offer light duty work while you recover, through L&I's Stay at Work program.

## 17. Drugs, Alcohol and Smoking

**Drugs and alcohol.** Don't work, drive a company vehicle or come to work impaired by alcohol, marijuana, illegal drugs or misused prescription drugs. No using, having, buying or selling them on the job, on Company property or in Company vehicles. If you take a prescription that could affect your safety, talk to your supervisor. Medical marijuana is generally not safe for field and shop work.

If you show up smelling of alcohol or marijuana, you'll be sent home. Sparrow may require a drug or alcohol test when there's reasonable suspicion. Violations may lead to termination. Sparrow may, at its discretion, offer a treatment program instead.

**Searches.** Sparrow may inspect Company property and vehicles at any time. With reasonable suspicion, we may ask to inspect bags or containers brought onto Company property. Refusing may lead to discipline.

**Smoking.** Sparrow is smoke-free. No smoking or vaping of tobacco or marijuana on Company property or in Company vehicles. On client sites, follow the client's rules. Excessive smoke breaks may lead to discipline.

## 18. Vehicles and Reimbursement

### Driving for work
If your job requires driving, you must have a valid driver's license and tell your supervisor right away if it's suspended or restricted.

### Company vehicles
Company vehicles are assigned at Sparrow's discretion. In a Company vehicle:
- Only you drive it. No passengers who aren't on Company business.
- No smoking, no open alcohol, and no drinking before driving.
- No handheld phone use.
- Park legally. Report tickets, damage and maintenance needs to your supervisor right away.
- If you buy fuel with your own card, submit the receipt. You'll be reimbursed on your next paycheck.

Personal use of a Company vehicle needs written permission from Mike. Return it with a full tank.

### Personal vehicles (mileage)
If you drive your own vehicle for approved work travel beyond your normal commute, like site to site, a supply run or a delivery, Sparrow pays the IRS mileage rate. For 2027, use the rate announced by the IRS in January. Log each trip on your timesheet with the date, from and to, purpose and miles. Submit by the end of the pay period the travel happened in. Late submissions aren't reimbursed.

### Travel and expenses
Get approval before traveling for work. Keep receipts and submit expenses within 30 days. Alcohol isn't reimbursed unless Mike approves it. Non-exempt employees are paid for work travel time as required by law.

## 19. Tools and Shop Use

### Your tools
Carpenters and cabinetmakers bring their own hand tools, tool belt or apron, and a basic cordless kit. Mark your tools. You're responsible for them. Keep tools clean and sharp. Replace dull carbide right away, since it's a safety issue. If a job needs a tool you don't have, tell your supervisor.

### Tool allowance
Full-time field employees get $500 a year for tools, bought through Sparrow accounts or cards. It doesn't carry over. Amounts over $500 are deducted from your paycheck under the deduction authorization you sign at hire.

### After-hours shop use
You can use the shop outside work hours only with permission from Mike or the shop manager. Before using it on your own, you must be trained and signed off on each machine you'll run, and sign a shop-use waiver. Personal projects are rented at $15 per hour. Sparrow's consumables are for Sparrow work only. Personal projects aren't covered by workers' comp.

### Work standards
Shop, millwork and installation standards are in the **Sparrow Shop and Field Standards** manual. Know them and follow them. If something is unclear, ask.
